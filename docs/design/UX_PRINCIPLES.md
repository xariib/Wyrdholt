# UX principles

## IMPLEMENTED — foundations to preserve

Portrait touch-first UI, native browser scrolling, safe-area handling, slot-based Gear sheets and queued build changes. Navigation does not stop activity. Technical save/clock control is not a normal player decision.

## DECIDED / NOT IMPLEMENTED — presentation target

One-thumb controls, minimum 48 CSS-pixel action targets, readable body copy and obvious Back/Close. Pending taps must cancel on vertical drag, including gestures begun over cards. No hover/right-click requirement. Avoid dense desktop grids and nested sheets.

Loadout → slot alternatives → comparison → equip should remain shallow. Preserve context so another item/slot can be reviewed quickly. Use CURRENT versus NEW stats plus clear signed differences, prominent effect text, rarity labels and lock/NEW cues. Never substitute Power Score or Equip Best for build judgment.

Adventure must name mode, exact Farm target and stage. Failure should show reached stage, enemy remainder, best progress and safe next decisions without pretending to know an optimal build. Important loot should precede routine currency totals; Common drops must not flood attention.

Dark forest fantasy: blue-green near-black, ivory, warm amber and restrained moss. Pixel-art-compatible, handcrafted, no excessive glow or ornamental AI-fantasy chrome. Approved art direction uses a zoomed-out combat stage with distance between hero and enemies; UI remains readable. Weapons, helmets and shields must persist in every idle/attack/hit frame. Concept art is reference, not production animation proof.

## OPEN — validation

Test 360×640, 390×844, 412×915 and 480×854, then verify touch, safe areas and text legibility on the REDMAGIC 11 Pro and on a physical iOS device. Current production pinch-zoom suppression and overlay accessibility need separate review. Static mock captures prove layout only, not live simulation, native accessibility or gesture correctness.

## DECIDED / NOT IMPLEMENTED — game interface, not a dashboard

Owner clarification after Phase 1: the sandbox may explore structure freely, but its card-heavy layout is not the production visual direction. The approved earlier pixel-fantasy references remain the visual target. Build a game interface, not a themed website or dashboard.

Use fewer containers. Let combat and world art carry more of the screen, with readable interface elements integrated around the scene. Preserve the approved wider combat framing and separation between hero and enemies; more art area does not mean enlarging sprites until they crowd each other. Keep equipment visible through attack/hit animation.

Reserve bordered cards for information that genuinely needs grouping, such as a comparison or a discrete reward. Do not wrap every heading, stat, action, progress indicator or scene in its own panel. Prefer shared surfaces, spacing, typographic hierarchy and restrained separators where they communicate the structure. Large touch targets and portrait readability still apply.

Before production integration, review the whole screen against the approved reference images, not only isolated components. Component names such as ZoneCard and ClassCard are organizational labels, not a requirement to draw borders or backgrounds. Phase 1 screenshots are structural evidence, not approved final art direction.
